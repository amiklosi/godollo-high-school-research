# Veresegyházi Katolikus Gimnázium (VERKA)

**Checked:** 15 October 2026 · **Target:** 2027/2028, entry after grade 8.

[Magyarul](verka.hu.md) · [Main comparison](../README.md) · [Score calculation](../scores/verka.md)

## Bottom line

**Add VERKA to the shortlist, especially 0001 humanities/English and 0003 science/English.** It combines conventional gymnasium education, advanced language teaching, a Christian community, and an explicit openness to students without religious practice or denominational ties. That last point makes it materially different from the admission barriers found at Premontrei, Református and EGA.

It is **not secular**, and its 50-point oral means it is not an oral-free safety option. Confirm the new guide, religious expectations and the actual morning commute before assigning a preference rank.

## Four-year programs: previous-cycle benchmark, not confirmed 2027 codes

The **2026/2027 brochure**, pp. 13–15, describes two four-year classes of 32 students each, split into language tracks:

| Code | Profile | First foreign language | 2026 places in Sulinavigátor |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **0001** | Humanities: Hungarian/history emphasis | English | **24** |
| **0002** | Humanities | German | **8** |
| **0003** | Science-oriented | English | **24** |
| **0004** | Science-oriented | German | **8** |

- Both classes have increased mathematics hours and split groups in mathematics and Digital Culture.
- Humanities additionally splits Hungarian and history; science additionally splits physics and chemistry.
- First foreign language: **five lessons weekly**, starting from **A2**, with ability-based groups. This is **not** a language-preparatory year or bilingual subject teaching.
- Advanced German groups require at least eight qualifying applicants under the previous guide.
- Second language choices: **English, German or French**, subject to grouping/availability.
- In years 11–12, students can prepare at advanced level in two chosen subjects.

**Fit:** 0001 suits an English/humanities interest; 0003 is worth comparing with TIG Digital Culture for a science-minded student, but **science + Digital Culture groups do not establish a specialist programming track**. Ask how much actual programming, algorithms, projects and robotics run.

## Religion: a genuinely different eligibility position

The **September 2026 pedagogical program**, section “Integrált hitéleti nevelés”, explicitly says the school accepts young people **open to Christian values but without denominational ties or religious practice**.

The previous admission guide, p. 30, reinforces this:

- students who are not yet practicing believers are welcome if open to Christian spirituality;
- a clergy recommendation is requested if the family belongs to a church and can provide one;
- families without a close Christian denominational affiliation can also be admitted if they accept the school's values, and **no recommendation is requested from them**.

The checked sources **do not establish an EGA-style mandatory baptism commitment**. This is not a promise of automatic eligibility/admission: reconfirm the position in writing for **2027/2028** and describe the family's situation honestly.

Daily life is still substantively Catholic: the current pedagogical program includes **two religious-education lessons per week in every year**, school Mass preparation and Christian ethical education. Ask exactly which Masses, retreats and other religious activities are compulsory, and how students without faith are treated in practice.

Source: [2026 pedagogical program](https://verka.hu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/VERKA-PP-2026.pdf), printed pp. 21–22 and 70–71; previous guide p. 30.

## Admission and oral preparation

Previous-cycle maximum: **50 school-grade + 100 centralized written + 50 oral = 200**.

For four-year applicants, grades come from **year-end grades 6 and 7, and the first semester of grade 8**. At each period, count:

1. the **average** of Hungarian grammar and literature;
2. mathematics;
3. history;
4. the relevant first foreign language (English for 0001/0003, German for 0002/0004);
5. one chosen subject from biology, geography, chemistry, physics or Digital Culture.

Sum and multiply by **2/3**. The chosen science/digital subject **need not be the same in each year**, according to the brochure FAQ. All 5s give **50/50**, assuming the required language grades exist; do not assume English grades qualify for a German track.

The oral includes Hungarian and the chosen foreign language, assessing comprehension, communication, cooperation, creativity, community/religious experience and application documents. English/German tasks include a short reading and picture-based conversation. The applicant writes an **approximately one-A4-page handwritten introduction letter** about family, interests, school experience and motivation. This is not the parent-written letter used for six-year applicants.

Documents in the previous cycle: KIFIR application, written result copy, certified school grades, introduction letter, and clergy recommendation where applicable. **Recheck 2027 deadlines and document rules.**

Sulinavigátor's **2026** figures:

| Track | Oral invitation /150 | Final cutoff /200 | Applicants / places | Written + oral needed with 50 grade points |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0001 humanities/English | 100 | **155.7** | 176 / 24 | **105.7** |
| 0002 humanities/German | 100 | **157** | 39 / 8 | **107** |
| 0003 science/English | 100 | **152** | 162 / 24 | **102** |
| 0004 science/German | 100 | **150.3** | 46 / 8 | **100.3** |

Thus 50 written points matched the recorded 2026 invitation threshold, **not final admission**. The older brochure describes earlier invitations as “around 105” before oral, not a permanent guaranteed rule. Small German intakes can produce volatile cutoffs; they are not automatically easier. Applicant/place ratios are not admission probabilities.

For the English tracks, a **70 written + 40 oral** scenario gives **160/200**, above the listed 2023–2026 final cutoffs. This is a planning scenario, **not a current official cutoff or guarantee**. [Detailed calculations and history](../scores/verka.md).

## Outcomes and public opinions

**School-reported evidence, not independently audited:** the 2026/2027 brochure, p. 10, says higher-education progression is around **90%**, **89%** finish with at least one language certificate, and **85%** were accepted to their first-choice destination. It does not provide enough cohort/denominator detail for direct comparison with TIG/Szerb or a current national ranking. The daytime school has a shorter graduation history than those established gymnasiums.

A small [Gyakori Kérdések thread](https://www.gyakorikerdesek.hu/kozoktatas-tanfolyamok__magyar-iskolak__11647115-veresegyhazi-katolikus-gimnazium-verka-velemeny), with January 2025 replies, is **mixed**:

- positive account: most teachers humane/reasonable, modern building, many programs;
- critical account: language teaching and hygiene complaints;
- both longer accounts describe a substantial **workload increase in grade 9**, after the first two years of the six-year program.

These are anonymous anecdotes, largely about six-year students. Do not generalize them into a verified picture of the four-year class. No independently verified current rank is assigned here.

## Shortlist recommendation / open-day questions

**Investigate alongside the conventional alternatives after TIG**, not as an automatic replacement for JPP's different model. For a non-practicing family open to a Christian school, VERKA may be a more realistic church-school candidate than the other researched options. Rank it only after the student has visited and commute/eligibility are confirmed.

Ask:

1. Are non-practicing/non-baptized students without clergy recommendations still accepted in 2027/2028? What exactly must the family commit to?
2. Which religious activities are compulsory, and how is disagreement or nonbelief handled?
3. What are the new codes, places, grade formula, oral marking criteria and dates?
4. How much programming is taught in 0003, and what distinguishes it from 0001 in actual weekly hours?
5. Which teachers will take the next grade-9 classes? How much homework and testing is typical?
6. How often do zero/eighth periods occur, and which bus services do commuting pupils use?
7. What are the last three cohorts' advanced-Matura and university outcomes, with denominators?
8. Are any contributions expected beyond tuition-free education, meals and optional trips?
